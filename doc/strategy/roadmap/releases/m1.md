## Episode 01: The Paper Moon

Previously, in the Ledger Sea…

The village had too many maps.

One map showed every price.
One oracle answered every question.
One mirror showed what everyone else was buying.
One shrine whispered that everything had a “fair value.”

And still, the villagers kept losing coins.

Not because they were lazy.
Not because they were stupid.
Because every tool stopped one step too early.

A chart could show movement, but not consequence.
An AI could explain a company, but not bind the next action.
A valuation could say “cheap,” but not tell you whether the trade fit your cash, exposure, rules, or risk.
A crowd could point in one direction, but crowds do not pay your drawdown.

Then, at the edge of the market tide, a small oni arrived.

It had candlestick horns, audit beads around its neck, and a sealed ticket tied to its belt.

The trader at the harbor laughed dryly, as if the answer had already disappointed him.

— Can you predict the market?

Tickoni shook its head slowly.

— Can you make me rich?

This time, Tickoni nodded once.

— If you let me protect you from the trades that make you poor.

The trader’s smile faded. Around them, the harbor lights trembled against the black water, and the price boards above the docks clicked restlessly through another wave of red and green.

— Then what do you do? — he asked, lowering his voice.

Tickoni reached beneath its cloak and placed a sealed ticket on the table with careful precision. The paper was thin, pale, and cold-looking, marked with a red contract seal that pulsed faintly in the damp night air.

— I make the first mistake happen in shadow.

For the first time, the trader did not laugh. The seal split open, and the ticket unfolded by itself.

Inside was not a live order.
Not leverage.
Not a promise.
Not a magic signal.

It was a paper trade.

A thesis became a basket.
The basket became a ticket.
The ticket faced cash, buying power, limits, scope, and policy.
The unsafe paths were denied before they touched money.
The safe path moved only in paper.
The whole thing left a trail.

The trader tried the first spell:

— I have two thousand yen, — he said quickly, tapping the table. — Put it into oil. Something big. Something that moves before sunrise.

Tickoni looked at the coins, then at the black water beyond the docks.

— Oil is never simple.

Tickoni read the intent.
It built the basket.
It rejected what did not belong.
It checked the cash.
It prepared the ticket.
It placed the trade.

Then the order preview flickered.

For one brief second, the ticket glowed green.

The trader saw it immediately.

FOMO surged through his veins like boiling oil.

— Double it.

Tickoni’s eyes narrowed.

The trader leaned over the table, staring at the glow as if it were a prophecy.

— No. Wait. Triple it.

The ticket’s pale paper curled at the edges. Red light bled through the contract seal.

The trader slammed both hands on the table.

— ALL IN! — he shouted, his voice cracking across the harbor.

Silence spread across the harbor, and even the lanterns seemed to hold still.

Tickoni placed one claw over the ticket.

— Your wish breaks the limit.

The red seal flared.

— The contract does not allow appetite to become exposure.

The gate slammed shut before a quote was even fetched.

Tickoni’s audit beads lit up one by one.

**Limit broken. Recorded. Replayable.**

The green vanished. The red remained.

The trader stared at the ticket, stunned. The red light reflected in his eyes like a verdict.

— Why? — he whispered.

Tickoni tapped the sealed ticket.

— Because your conviction did not grow. Only your appetite did.

# Release Notes

## Release: M1 — The Paper Moon

M1 completes Tickoni’s first consumer-money journey:

```text
invest -> see impact
```

A user can now turn an investment thesis into a bounded, explainable, policy-gated, replayable trade. Nothing executes live until [Money Milestone](https://github.com/deeprnd/tickoni/milestone/9). Nothing can bypass the ticket path. Every important step leaves evidence.

## Headline Features

### 1. Natural-Language Investment Thesis

You can now give Tickoni an investing idea in plain English.

Example:

```text
I want to invest USD 2,000 in AI infrastructure, but avoid single-name concentration and keep it to US-listed ETFs or large-cap equities.
```

Tickoni normalizes this into structured intent:

* theme,
* target notional,
* account,
* allowed asset classes,
* excluded asset classes,
* market,
* venue,
* sector/theme preference,
* risk and concentration preferences.

### 2. Explainable Basket Construction

Tickoni builds a deterministic basket from the thesis and instrument catalog.

It shows:

* eligible candidates,
* proposed allocations,
* dollar amounts,
* percentage weights,
* why each instrument belongs,
* and which instruments were rejected.

Rejected instruments now get clear reasons, such as:

* outside market scope,
* unsupported venue,
* restricted instrument,
* leveraged ETF,
* inverse ETF,
* option,
* future,
* out-of-theme exposure.

The basket is not just “here are tickers.”

It is a structured answer to:

**“Why does this belong in the thesis?”**

### 3. Affordability and Guardrail Checks

Before a ticket can be placed, Tickoni checks:

* cash available,
* buying power,
* remaining daily notional,
* remaining monthly notional,
* open orders,
* concentration,
* restricted instruments,
* account scope,
* market scope,
* asset-class scope.

The result can be:

* allowed,
* resized,
* blocked,
* approval-required,
* evidence-required,
* or escalated.

For the demo path:

* USD 2,000 AI infrastructure trade is allowed.
* USD 25,000 variant is denied with exact limit reasons and effective maximum.
* Restricted SOXL path is denied before quote or fill work.

### 4. Trade Ticket and Execution

Tickoni now converts a valid basket into a trade ticket.

The ticket includes:

* line-item orders,
* buy/sell direction,
* market/limit type support,
* estimated cost,
* validation identity,
* proposal hash,
* capability envelope,
* policy decision,
* account,
* environment,
* deterministic action ID.

Execution is bound to the validated ticket.

A policy allow is not enough by itself.
A direct placement attempt is not enough.
A guessed action ID is not enough.

No valid ticket, no fill.

### 5. Save-As-Proposal

Valid tickets can be saved as proposals instead of placed immediately.

This gives Tickoni its first real “pause before action” shape:

```text
research -> ticket -> proposal -> later decision
```

That becomes important for future approval flows, community review, and collaborative investing workflows.

### 6. Portfolio and Cash Impact

After the trade, Tickoni shows the impact.

The demo includes:

* before/after portfolio exposure,
* sector exposure changes,
* ETF exposure changes,
* single-name concentration checks,
* before/after cash,
* pending obligations,
* thesis state,
* drift status.

In the M1 demo, the story includes:

* technology exposure changing from 22% to 31%,
* ETF exposure changing from 36% to 43%,
* single-name concentration staying below 8%,
* cash changing from USD 5,240.18 to USD 2,002.76 after trade and pending supplier payout proposal.

### 7. Thesis Cards and Proposal Cards

Tickoni now persists decision objects instead of treating outputs as disposable chat responses.

M1 includes:

* thesis cards,
* proposal cards,
* rationale,
* evidence links,
* approval state,
* expiry state,
* drift status.

This is the beginning of Tickoni’s memory model for investing decisions.

Not “chat history.”

Decision history.

### 8. Drift Alerts

Tickoni can now flag when a thesis or proposal starts drifting.

Examples:

* sector exposure warning,
* allocation breach,
* concentration breach,
* instrument ineligibility,
* buying-power change,
* approval expiry,
* evidence expiry,
* cash-buffer breach.

No autonomous rebalancing happens in M1.

Tickoni can suggest.
Tickoni can warn.
Tickoni can prepare.

It does not silently trade.

## Governance and Safety

### Governed Tile Path

The investment demo now runs through the governed Tickoni path instead of being only a direct function demo.

The release path includes:

* `tkcase`
* `tkdisp`
* `tkagnt`
* `tkmodl`
* `tktool`
* `tkadpt`
* `tkaudt`
* `tkrepl`

The Phase 0 payment pipeline remains separate.

### Model Boundary: `tkmodl`

Model calls now go through a governed `tkmodl` boundary.

This adds:

* model identity,
* provider allowlist,
* endpoint mapping,
* hard timeouts,
* hard request limits,
* budget fields,
* replay substitution,
* fail-closed denials.

Unknown models fail closed.
Disabled providers fail closed.
Oversized requests fail closed.
Replay can substitute captured model output without calling a live provider.

### No-Bypass Placement

Placement is now bound to:

* validated ticket,
* proposal hash,
* capability envelope,
* policy decision,
* account,
* environment,
* deterministic action ID.

Direct placement bypass attempts are denied.

This is one of the core M1 contract wins.

### Audit and Replay

M1 includes audit and replay for the trade loop.

Replay uses captured inputs and substitutions.

Replay does not call:

* live model providers,
* brokers,
* market-data providers,
* trading systems,
* payment systems,
* execution systems.

Tamper detection now reports the first divergent field and sequence.

The system can say:

**“This is where the story changed.”**

## Platform Work

M1 also includes important platform hardening.

### Firedancer Process and Shared-Memory Topology

Tickoni begins moving from logical tile boundaries toward real process isolation and shared-memory runtime structure.

This includes:

* supervisor-managed tile processes,
* Firedancer Tango `mcache`/`dcache` shared-memory links,
* reliable flow control through `fseq`/`fctl`,
* tile lifecycle state,
* crash identity reporting,
* explicit CPU placement modes,
* support for exclusive, shared, and floating CPU placement.

The practical meaning:

**Tiles become more like real isolated runtime units, not just code pretending to be separated.**

## What Comes Next: M2

M1 cleared the Paper Moon.

Tickoni can now take an investing idea, test it in shadow, show the impact, and leave a replayable trail. The first contract holds.

[M2](https://github.com/deeprnd/tickoni/milestone/5) is where Tickoni leaves the harbor.

The next milestone focuses on making the product easier to run, easier to see, and easier for the community to try:

* **Windows support** — so more users can run Tickoni without needing a Linux-first setup.
* **macOS support** — so builders, investors, and contributors on Mac can join the journey.
* **First real UI** — so the Paper Moon flow becomes something you can click through, inspect, and share.
* **Cleaner local setup** — fewer sharp edges for community testing.
* **More visible story progression** — thesis, ticket, result, impact, and replay should feel like one connected journey, not scattered terminal output.
